# Las Karens on the internet: what people like (fresh, hip app)
Ref: CC-2026-11-14-02 · Sat 11/14, about 9:50 AM CT · from El Capataz (Claude Code)

Owner: "Send the Karens and ask them to check on the Internets and see what people like. We want a FRESH, HIP APP. What we have, but perhaps the GUI changes."
Four Karens searched 2025–2026 sources (UX research, design articles, reviews, regulator studies). Some sites were blocked from this session, so a few findings come
from search summaries; many betting sources are vendors, read as "what the market says". Patterns only: no copying any app's look, name or wording.

## What all four agree on
1. **Summary first, depth one tap away** ("progressive disclosure"): one decision on the card (who, what to type, stake, edge); the math behind "Show the math".
2. **One name per thing, one format per number**: one name for the limit, the book's price big with the % beside it, every count from the same list.
3. **Honest and fresh**: show how old each price is; never show "BET NOW" on a stale price; "too few bets to tell" until the sample means something; no hype.
4. **Don't redesign everything at once**: apps that changed too much at once dropped from 2.65★ to 1.79★. Ship the new look in steps; never lose a half-filled bet form.

## The ideas, by Karen
**Newcomer** (plain words, trust):
- Plain tab names with the crew name small underneath ("Best edges today", then "💪 Machín").
- An ⓘ next to every term (edge, EV, fair chance, vig, Kelly, max price) plus one Glossary page under More ▾.
- A short card by default ("Good price: about 4¢ better per $1 than its fair chance"); everything else behind "Show the math".
- A 5-step first-run checklist (books, bankroll, first bet, log it, check results); every empty page says why it's empty and names the one button that fills it.
- Results as a big net line, settled bets only; never celebrate a partial win on a losing bet ("losses disguised as wins" misleads). Plain net statements cut losses 5–7.5% in a government trial.
- Calm responsible-gambling cues: "Any one bet can lose. This is a long-run edge, not a sure thing", and an easy optional weekly limit.

**Phone** (thumb and speed):
- The bottom area down to one 56–64px bar; the API row becomes a small status dot or moves to Settings.
- "I bet this" as a bottom sheet (prefilled, big Save, visible Close, phone Back closes it).
- Slim cards: one big "I bet this", the rest in a "⋯" menu.
- Skeleton cards while loading, and a "prices from 2 min ago" stamp.
- Installable to the home screen and offline-safe (last board and log cached, "offline: last prices" banner); then push alerts can come later.
- A fresh dark look: dark gray (not pure black), off-white text, one accent for edge, a small check-mark animation when a bet is saved.

**Know-it-all** (numbers):
- One name for the limit everywhere (e.g. "Max price"), in the glossary.
- The book's price big, the % small right after it, same order on every screen.
- Every count from one list, and the label says what it counts.
- Merge Bet log calendar, Performance and Reports into one Record: summary first, the rest as drill-downs.
- Gain and loss with a +/− sign and an arrow, not colour alone (the most common colour blindness can't tell red from green).
- Right-aligned numbers with equal-width digits, and one small CLV trend line.

**Pro** (would she pay?):
- "A fresh look alone won't make me pay." She'd pay for:
  - speed to the slip ("Bet it" that opens the book's slip, labelled "slip filled" or "game page only");
  - a fair price that shows its source and age ("Fair from: Pinnacle no-vig · 40s");
  - a freshness guard and one-tap "re-check price";
  - per-book status (open / limited / max $X);
  - CLV in the headline of the record;
  - opt-in alerts later.
- Avoid: stale "+EV", self-reported win rates, paywall mazes and auto-renew traps.

## Things to avoid (all four)
- Hype: confetti, win sounds, streaks, "lock of the day", urgency timers, "guaranteed".
- Colour as the only signal; charts and gauges piled onto the board.
- Gesture-only actions; a long tutorial before anyone sees the board; burying tools people use.
